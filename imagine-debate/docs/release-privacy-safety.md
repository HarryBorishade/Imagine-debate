# Release: privacy and safety work

Status: working-tree changes, not a legal opinion or confirmation of App Store approval. The operator is an individual. The owner has not confirmed that the existing support email mailbox exists or is monitored. The intended initial audience includes ages 13-17; under-13 access is to be considered separately, including a possible school-managed mode. Live Supabase, hosting configuration, data retention, email delivery and production policies have not been verified. Do not publish the privacy policy as a final account of actual processing until the operator confirms it with a suitably qualified adviser.

## Implemented locally

- Privacy and cookie pages include reports, AI judging, analytics consent and the reporting notification provider; unverified 30-day deletion, full deletion and absolute security claims have been removed. A community-rules page is linked from signup, footer and sitemap.
- Debate messages submitted normally and timed-out drafts use the same server-side English word-list filter. A filtered timed-out draft is not sent and the room is told why. This filter can miss harmful language, flag harmless speech and be bypassed through unmoderated surfaces. It is not contextual moderation.
- The report function now saves reports even if email is unconfigured or notification fails; configure `REPORT_RECIPIENT`, `REPORT_FROM_EMAIL` and the email-provider secret securely before relying on notifications; review actual configuration and verify the report can be received. Email failure after saving leaves a report without a notification, so staff must also monitor the reports table. Account deletion invokes an Edge Function, but actual cascades, retained reports, transcripts, backups and third-party deletion are unverified.

## Before launch: owner and professional review

1. Confirm operator's legal name and contact details for the privacy notice, all actual hosting providers, data flows, international transfers and legal bases. Identify processors including debate hosting, Supabase, Google Gemini, analytics and report email; assess contracts, Google API data-use terms and transfer safeguards. Complete the policy with accurate retention periods and deletion processes for accounts, debates, reports, logs and backups. Confirm whether automated deletion exists; the 30-day dashboard filter is not a deletion guarantee. Review rights, complaint channels and age-specific notices with an adviser. Do not treat existing generic wording as a completed notice.
2. Plan for ages 13-17 initially and consider under-13s separately. Assess who is likely to access the service and the applicable UK online-safety and children's privacy duties. Decide and implement appropriate age assurance and safeguards before enabling access for minors; there is currently no age gate, reliable age assurance, or minor-specific protection.
3. Plan moderation operations: who reviews reports, how to prioritise imminent harm, when and how abusive content/accounts are removed or restricted, evidence retention, appeal/contact handling and staffing. Verify reporting works end to end and the support address is monitored. Add account blocking across room entry and future interactions; this is not implemented. Consider whether blocking should end a current room without penalising the reporter. Apple requires UGC apps to provide objectionable content filtering, reporting, blocking and contact information; Google Play also requires robust UGC moderation and in-app reporting/blocking as applicable. Verify current policies for the actual app and jurisdictions.
4. Close remaining moderation bypasses. Topics and descriptions are written directly to Supabase by the browser; usernames originate in Auth/profile data, and the message filter does not cover them. Move validation to trusted paths or use appropriately constrained database enforcement; validate every write surface, including API clients. Review direct message write grants and all permissive RLS policies in the live project. Do not rely on client-side checks. Assess obfuscation, languages, threats and harassment beyond a word list, false positives and review workflow. The server now serializes submissions within one process and retries a timed-out turn after a failed save; test multi-instance concurrency, restarts and persistent state before relying on moderation for enforcement.
5. Add reviewed terms of use or EULA and an acceptance mechanism appropriate to the business and target users. The community rules are behavioural guidance and signup links to them; this is not a recorded terms acceptance. Do not claim consent to the privacy policy or optional cookies merely because someone signs up.
6. Test account deletion through the actual app and live Supabase flows; verify downstream data and backup handling. Check Apple account deletion, privacy policy, data disclosure, age rating and app review requirements, plus Google Play data safety and deletion requirements if targeting Android. Confirm actual store account and app packaging; this repository is a web app, not a submitted native app.
7. Conduct required UK Online Safety Act applicability and risk assessments for user-to-user content; ask an appropriate adviser to assess scope and duties. Implement appropriate reporting, complaints and moderation processes for the chosen market. No document or filter can guarantee immunity from legal claims.

## Youth and schools: product direction, not a launch-ready implementation

The owner wants ages 13-17 considered first, with under-13s and a potential school-managed experience assessed separately. Do not treat a date-of-birth checkbox as proof of age or claim that minors can use the current service safely. The existing app permits one-to-one interaction through a four-digit code, has no account blocking and does not yet have age-specific access controls or a staffed moderation system.

- Before allowing minors to debate, complete a children's access assessment, a child-safety risk assessment, a data protection impact assessment and appropriate legal review for the jurisdictions served. Assess grooming, sexual content, bullying, harmful topics, adult-to-minor contact, small room-code enumeration, re-contact, fake school identities and moderator access.
- Design privacy by default: minimal age data, participant-only access verified against actual RLS, no public profiles or discovery for children by default, and strict controls on contact and access to debate history. Avoid sending sensitive personal information in arguments or to AI providers without a reviewed basis and appropriate controls.
- Develop a school-managed mode separately: verified organisations and educators, supervised classrooms, invitations scoped to an approved class, age-appropriate topics, stronger room access controls, effective reporting and blocking, access logs, and practical human review and safeguarding escalation. Any educator or guardian access to a child's data needs a clear role, notice, access scope and legal basis.
- Make reporting and blocking available during and after a debate, prevent blocked accounts from rejoining or contacting one another, preserve evidence where appropriate, and provide trained reviewers with clear response and escalation processes. Define fair handling for interruptions and ratings. Filters alone cannot catch the risks.
- Design a neutral age-entry and proportionate age-assurance approach only after assessing the risk and current legal requirements. TikTok generally requires users to be at least 13 and does not offer direct messaging to 13-15-year-olds; one-to-one debate rooms are interactive and should not be presented as equivalent without a separate risk assessment. Sources: https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/account-privacy-settings/privacy-and-safety-settings-for-users-under-age-18 and https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/protection-of-children-duties-under-the-online-safety-act
- Create and test a mailbox under the operator's control before publishing it as a support or privacy contact. The public support email is displayed only when configured, and the fixed two-business-day promise has been removed. Report notifications now require a configured sender and recipient as well as the email-provider secret; until those are configured and tested, the operator must monitor the reports table.
